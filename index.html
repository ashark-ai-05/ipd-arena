A lab for cooperation

In a one-shot prisoner’s dilemma, defection is the rational move. When the same players meet again and again, that changes. This app is a visual workbench for that repeated game: tournaments, inspectable match tapes, noisy observations, and ecological succession.

It illustrates findings from Robert Axelrod’s 1980 tournaments — that successful strategies tended to be nice, forgiving, retaliatory, and clear — and that there is no single best rule. The best play depends on the field. Noise can turn Tit for Tat against itself; a little extra forgiveness can break the spiral. This is a demonstration, not a claim of new science, and it makes no claims about markets or returns.
